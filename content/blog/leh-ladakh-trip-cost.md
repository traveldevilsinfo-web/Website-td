Seasonally, it breaks down like this (one way, per person):

- **Off-peak (roughly October to April):** fares near the ₹4,500 to ₹7,000 mark are common if you book a few weeks out. Kayak lists January as the cheapest month on average; several booking sites flag October as good value.
- **Peak (June to mid-September):** Kayak lists June as the most expensive month. Expect ₹10,000 to ₹20,000 one way, and more if you book in the last week.
- **Shoulder (May, late September):** anywhere between the two.

So a return ticket is roughly **₹9,000 to ₹14,000 off-peak** (₹4,500 to ₹7,000 × 2) and **₹20,000 to ₹40,000 in peak season** (₹10,000 to ₹20,000 × 2). These are ranges we observed, not quotes. **Fares change daily**, so check a few booking sites on your actual dates.

A few practical points. The direct flight takes about 1 hour 10 minutes to 1 hour 25 minutes, and IndiGo, Air India and SpiceJet all fly the route. Leh's airport sits at about 3,256 m, and flights there usually operate in the morning because afternoon winds make landing harder, so plan your Delhi departure for an early start.

## Local transport: taxi union rates and bike rentals

### Taxis

Sightseeing inside Ladakh runs on local union taxis. Widely reported local rules say taxis and self-drive cars from outside Ladakh can bring you to Leh but can't be used for sightseeing within the region. The union publishes a fixed rate card each season, and published 2026 lists show no increase on last season.

Union rates per vehicle (not per person), as published for 2026:

| Airport to Leh | ₹850 | ₹850 | ₹935 |
| Leh local (Hall of Fame, Magnetic Hill, Sangam, Pathar Sahib), full day | ₹3,238 | ₹3,408 | ₹3,749 |
| Leh – Nubra – Turtuk – Pangong – Leh, 4 days | ₹28,436 | ₹29,933 | ₹32,926 |

These rates include toll tax, parking and the driver's allowance.

For our route, one vehicle for the week costs:
- Cheapest: ₹28,436 + ₹3,238 + (₹850 × 2 airport runs) = **₹33,374**
- Most comfortable: ₹32,926 + ₹3,749 + (₹935 × 2) = **₹38,545**

Split four ways, that's **₹8,344 to ₹9,636 per person**. Split between two people, it's **₹16,687 to ₹19,273**. A solo traveller pays the whole ₹33,374 to ₹38,545 alone. This is where DIY Ladakh gets expensive for solo travellers.

## Inner Line Permit and environment fees

This line causes the most confusion, so here's what the official LAHDC Leh permit portal ([lahdclehpermit.in](https://www.lahdclehpermit.in/)) actually says:

- **Indian citizens don't need an Inner Line Permit.** The portal's FAQ states the inner line permit applies only to foreign tourists. Foreign nationals need a Protected Area Permit for places like Pangong, Nubra and Khardung La.
- **Domestic tourists do pay a fee.** It combines an environmental (green) fee, a Red Cross Fund contribution and a wildlife fee, and you can pay it online on the same portal. You don't need to visit a government office.
- **Booking through a local registered travel agent gets 10% off** the total fee, per the portal.
- The portal also warns about **fake environment fee slips**, so pay only on the official site or through a registered agent.

We couldn't find the exact rupee amounts on the official portal or the Leh district site (leh.nic.in), and travel blogs quote different figures. So our honest answer: **a few hundred rupees; check the official portal** before you go. In the totals above we set aside ₹1,000 as a safe allowance so you aren't caught short.

Our trip's inclusions list "all inner line permits for the trip". If you're booking with us, ask us to confirm on your batch whether the domestic environment and wildlife fee is covered.

## Food and the extras people forget

**Food.** Everything in Ladakh travels a long way to get there, so meals cost more than in Delhi. A March 2026 cost guide puts a simple thali at ₹200 to ₹300, café meals at ₹300 to ₹600, and a normal day's eating in Leh at ₹700 to ₹1,000. Expect meals in Nubra and Pangong to cost 20 to 40% more, with fewer options.

- **Budget:** ₹700 to ₹1,000 × 7 days = **₹4,900 to ₹7,000**
- **Mid-range (café meals):** ₹1,000 to ₹1,800 × 7 = **₹7,000 to ₹12,600**

**Extras.** On our itinerary, the Bactrian (double-hump) camel ride and ATV rides at the Hunder dunes are **on personal expense**, and they would be on a DIY trip too:

- **Camel ride:** reported prices range from about ₹200 for a short 15-minute ride up to ₹1,000 for longer rides, depending on duration and season.
- **ATV ride:** traveller reviews put it at around ₹1,000 per ride. Rates are set on the spot, so confirm before you get on.

Monument entry tickets and parking during sightseeing are small but add up, and they aren't included on our trip either.

## Group trip vs doing it yourself: a fair comparison

Looking at the totals above, the DIY budget column's lower end (₹17,844) is cheaper than our trip's ground cost (₹21,149 and up). So when does each option make sense?

**DIY wins when:**
- You're a group of four who already know each other and will split a taxi. That's how the budget column reaches ₹8,344 per person for transport.
- You're happy in dorms and basic homestays.
- You want your own pace, like an extra night at Pangong, a detour to Hanle, or a slow morning.

**A group trip wins when:**
- **You're solo or a pair.** A solo traveller's taxi alone costs ₹33,374 to ₹38,545. A couple pays ₹16,687 to ₹19,273 each just for transport, which is close to our whole trip price.
- **You want the planning done.** Stays in Leh, Nubra and Pangong, most meals, the vehicle, tolls and parking, an oxygen cylinder in the car, a first aid kit and a Team Captain are all arranged.
- **You'd rather not coordinate bookings** across Leh, Nubra and Pangong during peak season yourself.

What you give up on a group trip: fixed dates (we depart every Friday), shared rooms (double, triple or quad sharing, with double sharing at extra cost) and a set itinerary.

One thing both options should share: **acclimatisation.** The official Leh permit portal advises at least 48 hours of acclimatisation before heading to high-altitude areas, and no exertion for the first two days. Leh sits at around 3,500 m. Our itinerary keeps Day 1 for rest and Day 2 for gentle local sightseeing before crossing Khardung La on Day 3. If you plan your own trip, don't cut those days to save money.

## Ways to save on a Ladakh trip

1. **Fly off-peak or book early.** Flights are the biggest variable. Choosing between ₹9,000 and ₹40,000 on return fares matters more than any other decision you make.
2. **Fill the taxi.** Taxi rates are per vehicle, so four people pay a quarter each. Solo travellers can find co-travellers at hostels in Leh, or join a group trip.
3. **Book the full circuit as one package.** The 4-day union rate for Leh – Nubra – Turtuk – Pangong – Leh (₹28,436 to ₹32,926) works out cheaper than booking each leg on its own.
4. **Pay the environment fee through a registered local agent** for the 10% discount, and never pay a stranger for a paper slip.
5. **Eat where locals eat.** Thalis and momos cost a fraction of what the cafés charge.
6. **Skip the paid extras you don't care about.** A camel ride and an ATV ride together can add ₹1,200 to ₹2,000.
7. **Don't economise on acclimatisation.** An extra night in Leh is cheap insurance against altitude sickness.

## FAQs

**How much does a 7-day Leh Ladakh trip cost from Delhi?**
Working from sourced prices, a budget DIY trip costs about ₹17,800 to ₹26,600 per person after you land. Add ₹9,000 to ₹14,000 for off-peak return flights, or ₹20,000 to ₹40,000 in peak season. Our group trip costs ₹19,949 including GST, plus flights, lunches and personal extras.

**What is the Delhi to Leh flight price?**
In September 2026, booking sites advertised one-way fares from about ₹4,900. Typical off-peak fares are around ₹4,500 to ₹7,000, and peak summer fares run ₹10,000 to ₹20,000 or more. Fares change daily, so check on your dates.

**Do Indians need an Inner Line Permit for Ladakh?**
No. The official LAHDC Leh permit portal says the inner line permit applies only to foreign tourists. Indian visitors pay a combined environment, Red Cross and wildlife fee online instead.

**What is the Ladakh permit fee?**
For domestic tourists it's a few hundred rupees. We couldn't confirm the exact current amount on the official portal, so check [lahdclehpermit.in](https://www.lahdclehpermit.in/) before you travel. Booking through a registered local agent gets you 10% off.

**Is it cheaper to rent a bike or take a taxi?**
For a solo rider, usually a bike: a Himalayan 411 costs ₹10,000 to ₹12,500 for five days before fuel, against ₹33,000+ for a taxi on your own. For four people sharing a taxi (about ₹8,300 to ₹9,600 each), the gap closes, and you get a warm seat over Khardung La.

**Can I use a car or bike rented in Delhi or Manali for sightseeing in Ladakh?**
Widely reported local rules say no. Outside vehicles can get you to Leh, but sightseeing within Ladakh has to be in a local taxi or on a locally rented bike. Confirm the current rules before you drive up.
